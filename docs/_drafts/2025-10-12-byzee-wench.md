### Overview


While watching tutorials on YouTube, I came across this [Byzee Wench Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pj1j7gsGTuE) by [Aussie Maille](https://www.youtube.com/@AussieMaillers) and decided to make it myself. This member of the [European]({{ site.baseurl }}{{ site.family_page }}#European) weave family is a fun variant of the [Byzantine]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url 2023-01-28-byzantine %}) weave. Unfortunately, I am unable to say who first documented the weave, as I have not been able to find it on [M.A.I.L.](https://www.mailleartisans.org/) or the [Chainmailers Forum](https://chainmaillers.com/).


### Materials

The rings used for the sample piece shown in this post are 16 {% include abbreviations/swg.html %} with a 1/4" {% include abbreviations/id.html %} for an {% include abbreviations/ar.html %} of 4.03 made of Bright Aluminum that I bought from the [Ring Lord](https://theringlord.com/).


### Notes

The Byzee Wench weave is fairly easy to understand and quite easy to make. I find it aesthetically pleasing, with its central line and protrusions giving it a distinctive look. As a chain weave, it works well for bracelets or loose necklaces, and the protrusions make it especially useful for attaching other items. The protrusions make it a strong starting point for charm bracelets or as a foundation for more complex projects. Since it is simple to learn, visually appealing, and versatile, I recommend learning to make this weave.
